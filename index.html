Walk the Trailer

The trailer, openable. Orbit it, then press W and you are inside at eye height for a six foot man — 67 in off the deck — with the walk clamped to the cells a body actually fits in, so you cannot walk through the galley here because you cannot walk through it there. The crosshair names whatever is in front of you, how far off it is, and why it was put there. Eleven activities and nineteen reach stations are in the menu: choosing one poses the body the collision test was run against, arms solved rather than illustrated, and opens the structure to a ghost so you can see him in it.

Ingold Trailer

The 8'-6" × 20'-0" trailer from the reference sheets, actually built — 407 members, 558 joints, 7,050 lb, five headed openings, a bath, galley, dinette and bed, and water, waste and off-grid power as connected systems. Press play and watch it being made, move by move, with what the building answered at each step.

Hospital

A diagnostic bay for buildings. Admit any model in the repository — a built world with a full chart, or a bare mesh in STL, Collada or glTF — and run the same instruments on it: an escape scan that fills the inside with light and plots where it gets out on the building unfolded flat, a CT that floods the volume with air and slices the result, radiographs, per-part exposure, and a contact sheet. Calibrated first against a box known to be sealed, because that is the only reading that can tell the building is wrong from our eyes are wrong.

Ant Scout

A colony of stigmergic ants, moved off the ground and onto a building. They crawl its surfaces, fire a small lidar fan from wherever they are standing, and forage for the places the geometry gives out — daylight, clashes, cliffs, pairs the fastening schedule says should be nailed and are not. Nothing is centrally planned: an ant that finds something carries it home and lays a trail, and the trail is evidence, not an order — every ant has its own threshold for believing it, and it evaporates, so a rumour nobody corroborates costs nothing to forget. Take a wall panel off and watch the colony converge on the hole, with the deterministic checks and the ray survey running alongside for comparison.

What's Fucked

Shaken at 0.8 g, rained on, weighed, photographed from twelve angles and again part by part, and filled with light to see where the light gets out — against a box known to be sealed, so a leak can be told from a broken instrument. The default state of a build is not “probably correct”. This one photographs itself from twelve named views — front, rear, sides, plan, three interiors, the underside, an x–ray — assumes every picture is wrong, and says why. Two agents with separate context windows: a critic that only accuses, and a builder that only acts. The score is the worst view, never the average, and a clean view does not finish anything — it moves the camera.

Operative Builder

A Three.js building environment that answers back. Instructions become framing operations; the framing reports what the instruction did to it — cut studs, over-sized bores, spans, missing bearing, a roof that no longer meets its walls — and every member can say how it became what it is.
