What the colony believes

Ranked by corroboration, not by loudness. One ant that saw something once is a rumour. Four ants that arrived separately are a defect.

Three instruments, same building

What this is

The formicary engine's ants, moved off the ground and onto a building. They crawl its surfaces, fire a small lidar fan from wherever they are standing, and forage — not for food, but for the places the geometry gives out. A ray that leaves the building is daylight. A ray that starts inside another member is a clash. A surface that ends at nothing is a cliff. Two members in contact that the fastening schedule says should be joined, and are not, is an unjoined pair.

Nothing here is centrally planned. An ant that finds something carries it back to the nest and lays pheromone on the way, and that trail is evidence, not an order: every ant has its own threshold for believing it and follows it only so far before striking out again. The trail evaporates, so a rumour nobody corroborates costs nothing to forget. That is the whole false-positive filter, and it is free.

The colony is not the only instrument, and it is not the best one at anything. The deterministic checks are exact and blind to whatever nobody wrote a rule for. The ray survey is exhaustive and says nothing about which member is at fault. The ants are neither, and they go where nobody sent them. The panel above runs all three so you can see one find what the other two missed.
